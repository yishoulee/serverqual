# Hypothesis Quality Audit

> **Set-level judgment: POOR.**  
> This is an editorial assessment of the quality of the hypothesis formulation in the ServerQual project record. It is **not** a measurement of the underlying markets.

![Hypothesis quality distribution](../assets/hypothesis-quality.svg)

## Why the set was weak

The central problem was not only that commercial validation was incomplete. The hypothesis set itself was badly designed.

1. **The project started from a technical category rather than one paid event.** "Qualification is messy" is a credible domain observation. It does not specify one buyer, one trigger, one costly consequence, or one purchase decision.
2. **Many hypotheses repeated the same unproven purchase assumption.** Different service ideas still depended on one core leap: that a customer would trust an independent newcomer's evidence, act on it, and pay for it.
3. **Twenty hypotheses were too many for the available discovery capacity.** The project completed one dedicated consultation plus limited field conversations. That evidence base could not discriminate twenty markets or service models.
4. **The register mixed different logical levels.** Some entries were customer jobs, some were deliverables, some were technical specialties, some were product concepts, and one was a capital/operating model.
5. **Falsification thresholds were mostly implicit.** Most ideas did not begin with a time-boxed behavioral threshold such as a qualified buyer sharing a live artifact, introducing a budget owner, accepting a scoped proposal, or paying a pilot.
6. **Pricing and financial modeling arrived before purchase evidence.** Those models can test feasibility, but they do not create evidence of demand.
7. **Several ideas violated the founder/resource boundary.** Cross-OEM testing, air-gapped work, AI full-stack qualification, specialist tuning, and a shared lab demand hardware access, security trust, specialist depth, relationships, or capital that were not available to a solo new entrant.
8. **The no-go should have arrived earlier.** Once buyer acceptance, trust, purchasing authority, and a differentiated wedge remained unresolved, the efficient move was to narrow to one lane or stop.

## Individual audit

| ID | Hypothesis | Quality | Candid assessment |
|---|---|---:|---|
| H01 | Exact-stack change qualification | **Moderate** | Clear trigger and bounded output, but buyer, approver, and budget owner were not separated. It still assumed an independent dossier could influence a real approval decision. |
| H02 | Certification-readiness outsourcing | **Weak** | Plausible deadline and user, but it depended on vendors outsourcing sensitive pre-certification work, granting access, and trusting an unknown provider despite official programs and established labs. |
| H03 | BOM / component substitution | **Moderate** | One of the cleaner formulations: concrete change and potentially costly consequence. It still lacked a fixed segment, economic threshold, acceptance owner, and explicit paid test. |
| H04 | Evidence-pack production | **Poor** | Starts from an artifact rather than a blocked customer decision. It assumed packaging logs and sources creates a separate budget before proving that the package changes anything. |
| H05 | Vendor-neutral incident reproduction | **Weak** | The pain can be real, but incidents are episodic and multi-party. The natural purchasing path often runs through vendor support or an integrator. |
| H06 | Supportability-drift monitoring | **Poor** | A recurring product was proposed before a recurring paid problem was established. No costly drift event or clear inventory owner was identified. |
| H07 | Emergency firmware-security qualification | **Weak** | Urgency increases trust, access, liability, and response-time requirements, making an unknown solo provider particularly hard to buy during an emergency. |
| H08 | Virtualization / HCI upgrade | **Weak** | Understandable trigger, but the scope is heavy and vendors, integrators, and internal teams already occupy the decision path. No independent wedge was defined. |
| H09 | Air-gapped qualification / provenance | **Poor** | The segment magnifies security, data-handling, and trust barriers. It is structurally unattractive for a new entrant without an existing relationship and access model. |
| H10 | Legacy / end-of-life bridge | **Weak** | Continuity pain is plausible, but the observed case pointed toward small budgets, local supplier trust, and accountability for a working system rather than a separate qualification dossier. |
| H11 | Procurement / delivery acceptance | **Moderate** | A concrete order/BOM discrepancy and acceptance authority make this legible. The weakness is channel ownership: the value may belong inside the supplier or integrator contract. |
| H12 | Workload performance regression | **Moderate** | Measurable threshold and operational consequence make this testable, but it requires workload access, credible performance expertise, and a buyer willing to separate the work from existing engineering. |
| H13 | AI full-stack qualification | **Poor** | Too broad, hardware-heavy, and credibility-intensive. "AI full-stack" is a technology category, not a sufficiently narrow buyer problem. |
| H14 | Redfish / BMC conformance regression | **Moderate** | Technically specific and testable, but stand-alone value is weak because free tools and internal regression are strong substitutes. It needed proof of repeated costly customer-impacting breaks. |
| H15 | Component cross-OEM qualification | **Poor** | Requires access to multiple platforms, common test plans, and vendor trust. It drifts toward a capital- and relationship-intensive lab before buyer pull exists. |
| H16 | New OS-release readiness | **Moderate** | Clear release trigger and strong fit with prior experience, but OEM/OS-vendor programs and internal teams already own much of this work. Overflow demand had to be proven directly. |
| H17 | Edge / remote-site qualification | **Weak** | Plausible pain, but the segment, decision owner, and economic consequence were too vague. It easily collapses into generic systems-integration consulting. |
| H18 | Shared qualification laboratory | **Poor** | This is an operating/capital model, not a customer hypothesis. It should only be considered after repeated paid demand. |
| H19 | BIOS / power / thermal / NUMA tuning | **Poor** | A distinct specialist performance-engineering business with competence and outcome risk; it dilutes rather than sharpens the core thesis. |
| H20 | Cross-OEM compatibility intelligence | **Poor** | A data/monitoring product introduced before action-linked demand. Free sources, maintenance burden, and unclear willingness to pay make it weak without a specific recurring decision. |

## Distribution

- **Poor:** 8
- **Weak:** 6
- **Moderate:** 6
- **Strong:** 0

The six Moderate hypotheses were not validated businesses. "Moderate" only means they were better-formed and more testable than the rest.

## Correct close-out

> **Useful failed exploration; weak hypothesis design; limited external discovery; no commercial validation; correct decision to stop before significant capital spend.**

The strongest part of ServerQual was the eventual stop discipline. The weakest part was allowing a broad technical domain to expand into a twenty-hypothesis portfolio before proving one purchase path.
