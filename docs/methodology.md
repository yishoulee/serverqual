# Research Methodology

ServerQual was run as a bounded validation project rather than as a product-development project.

## 1. Desk research

The initial research screened more than 70 official technical pages covering server platforms, operating-system support, certification programs, firmware, drivers, and validation ecosystems.

The purpose was to understand what vendors already provide before assuming that an external service was needed.

## 2. Workflow mapping

The project decomposed qualification work into recurring activities such as:

- configuration capture
- compatibility and support research
- test planning
- regression testing
- evidence collection
- defect reproduction
- vendor escalation
- retesting
- release/change decisions

This helped separate the technical problem from the commercial opportunity.

## 3. Practitioner discovery

Practitioner interviews were used to test whether the observed problems also appeared in real operational environments.

Topics included:

- documentation quality
- hardware and motherboard variation
- Linux driver age and compatibility
- component replacement
- support expectations
- procurement constraints
- small-budget environments
- preference for trusted local suppliers

Interview evidence was treated as directional rather than as proof of market demand.

## 4. Field research

Industry-event observations in Taiwan were used to compare assumptions against vendors operating in industrial, embedded, and configurable computing markets.

The purpose was not to count booth conversations as customer validation. Field observations were used to identify:

- where OS validation visibly mattered
- where compatibility and lifecycle issues appeared
- which market segments were poor fits
- which commercial or productization questions needed deeper investigation

## 5. Competitive and substitute analysis

ServerQual was compared against existing alternatives including:

- server OEM qualification teams
- OS and silicon-vendor certification programs
- internal customer validation teams
- ODM/OEM engineering organizations
- established third-party laboratories
- system integrators and vendor support organizations

The project treated these groups as substitutes even when they did not sell an identical service.

## 6. Commercial validation standard

A credible business would have required stronger evidence such as:

- repeated buyer conversations
- a design partner
- a paid pilot
- a repeatable deliverable
- willingness to accept independent evidence
- a differentiated entry point

Those conditions were not reached.

## Evidence limits

This project was not a statistically representative market study.

It was an early-stage validation effort intended to answer a narrower decision:

**Was there enough evidence to justify further investment?**

The answer was no.
